# Trifecta Program — 40-Week HYROX and Bodybuilding Redesign

This document replaces the final 40 weeks of the larger Trifecta macrocycle.

- **Weeks 1–20 of the full program:** Powerlifting preparation and competition
- **Weeks 21–40:** HYROX preparation, with competition in Week 40
- **Weeks 41–60:** Bodybuilding preparation, with competition in Week 60

The HYROX block is designed for a strength-dominant athlete exiting a powerlifting peak with accumulated overuse symptoms. Running and conditioning become the primary adaptations. Hypertrophy remains, but most general strength work shifts toward unilateral, contralateral, tempo-controlled and structural exercises. Bilateral work is retained where it is required for race specificity.

---

# Programming Principles

## HYROX is a running event with strength stations

The race contains eight 1 km runs separated by eight stations. The program therefore prioritizes:

1. Running frequency and tissue tolerance
2. Aerobic capacity and threshold development
3. Compromised running after stations
4. Station economy and transition skill
5. Strength and muscle retention
6. Injury control

This is not powerlifting with circuits added. Running development is the center of the block and strength supports it.

## Structural strength distribution

General strength work should shift across the block as follows:

| Phase | Unilateral/structural work | Bilateral/race-specific work |
|---|---:|---:|
| Early HYROX block | 75–80% | 20–25% |
| Middle HYROX block | 60–70% | 30–40% |
| Final six weeks | 45–55% | 45–55% |

Unilateral work rebuilds the chassis. Sleds, carries, wall balls, loaded lunges and ergs prepare the athlete to race.

## Intensity distribution

Most conditioning should remain easy enough to support volume.

- 75–85% low intensity
- One dedicated running quality session most weeks
- One compromised-running or race-specific session most weeks
- No more than two genuinely hard conditioning days in a standard week
- Every fourth week reduces running and lower-body volume by roughly 15–25%

# Standard HYROX Training Week

## Monday — Easy run and structural lower body

### Run

- 30–60 minutes at conversational intensity
- Add 4–8 × 15-second strides once tissues tolerate faster running

### Strength

1. Rear-foot-elevated split squat — 4 × 6–10/side, `31X1`
2. Split-stance RDL — 3–4 × 8–10/side, `3111`
3. Front-foot-elevated reverse lunge — 3 × 8–12/side
4. Single-leg seated hamstring curl — 3 × 10–15
5. Single-leg calf raise — 4 × 8–15
6. Tibialis raise — 3 × 15–25
7. Copenhagen plank — 3 × 20–40 seconds/side

## Tuesday — Running intervals and upper structural strength

### Running options by phase

- 8–12 × 400 m
- 5–8 × 800 m
- 4–6 × 1 km
- 3–5 × 6 minutes
- 3 × 10 minutes at threshold
- 2 × 20 minutes at controlled threshold

### Upper body

1. Half-kneeling single-arm landmine press — 4 × 6–10/side
2. One-arm dumbbell row — 4 × 8–12/side
3. Single-arm incline dumbbell press — 3 × 8–12/side
4. Half-kneeling pulldown — 3 × 10–12/side
5. Chest-supported rear-delt row — 3 × 12–15
6. Serratus cable press or wall slide — 3 × 12–15
7. Single-arm overhead carry — 3 × 30–50 m/side

## Wednesday — HYROX strength EMOM

### Base option: 30-minute alternating EMOM

Repeat six rounds:

- Minute 1: Sled push, 10–15 m
- Minute 2: Sled pull, 10–15 m
- Minute 3: Eight front squats at RPE 6–7
- Minute 4: 10–14 calories SkiErg
- Minute 5: Rest or easy walk

### Specific option: 36-minute EMOM

Repeat six rounds:

- Minute 1: 12–15 calories SkiErg
- Minute 2: Heavy sled push, 12.5 m
- Minute 3: 10–12 calories row
- Minute 4: 12 wall balls
- Minute 5: 10–12 m burpee broad jumps
- Minute 6: Rest

Finish most working minutes with 10–20 seconds remaining. EMOMs are density tools, not technique failure tests.

## Thursday — Recovery or off-feet aerobic work

Choose one:

- 35–60 minutes easy bike
- 30–45 minutes elliptical
- Easy swimming
- 20–30 minutes recovery jog
- Full rest when lower-leg or hip symptoms are elevated

Optional structural work:

- Hip mobility
- Foot and ankle strengthening
- Soleus work
- Isometric adduction
- Light trunk rotation and anti-rotation

## Friday — Full-body functional bodybuilding

### A1/A2

- Front-foot-elevated split squat — 4 × 8/side
- One-arm neutral-grip pulldown — 4 × 10/side

### B1/B2

- B-stance hip thrust — 3 × 10/side
- Single-arm dumbbell bench press — 3 × 10/side

### Structural circuit — three to four rounds

- 10 lateral step-downs/side
- 12 cable rows/side
- 12 hamstring curls
- 15–20 lateral raises
- 30–40 m suitcase carry/side

### Optional aerobic finisher — 8–12 minutes at RPE 6–7

- 10 calories SkiErg
- Eight alternating dumbbell lunges
- 10 ring rows
- 30–45 seconds easy movement

## Saturday — Long run or compromised-running session

### Base-phase option

- 45–90 minutes easy running, progressed gradually

### Sled transition

Five rounds:

- 800 m at controlled race pace
- 15–20 m heavy sled push
- 800 m
- 15–20 m sled pull
- Rest 2–3 minutes

### Burpee transition

Four to six rounds:

- 1 km at goal race pace
- 20 m burpee broad jumps
- 90 seconds easy recovery

### Lunge transition

Four rounds:

- 800 m run
- 25 m sandbag walking lunges
- 400 m immediately after lunges
- Rest two minutes

### Wall-ball closing session

Three rounds:

- 1 km run
- 25 wall balls
- 500 m faster than race pace
- Rest three minutes

## Sunday — Complete rest

Walking and light mobility are acceptable. Do not convert this into another training session.

# HYROX Phase Details

## Weeks 21–23 — Powerlifting exit and tissue restoration

- No maximal bilateral loading
- No grinding repetitions
- Most work at RPE 7 or lower
- No conventional deadlift required
- No back squat required
- Use goblet squats, light front squats, hip thrusts and machines as tolerated
- Two or three easy runs
- One light interval exposure
- One off-feet aerobic session
- No full simulations

## Weeks 24–27 — Aerobic and structural base

Primary exercises:

- Bulgarian split squat
- Split-stance RDL
- Reverse lunge
- High step-up
- Single-leg press
- Single-arm press
- Single-arm row
- Contralateral carries

Minimum bilateral dose:

- Front squat — 3 × 5–8 once weekly
- Sled push/pull technique weekly
- 30–60 wall balls weekly
- One farmer's-carry exposure weekly

## Weeks 28–31 — Threshold development

Weekly priorities:

1. Threshold intervals
2. Long easy run
3. Two easy aerobic runs
4. One small compromised-running exposure

Reduce lower-body hypertrophy volume by approximately 15–20% while retaining:

- One heavy Bulgarian split-squat day
- One moderate split-stance RDL day
- One bilateral front-squat or sled stimulus
- Two to three calf/soleus exposures
- Two upper-body hypertrophy exposures

## Weeks 32–35 — HYROX integration

Prioritize the transitions most likely to disrupt running mechanics.

### Sled push to run

- 400–800 m run
- Sled push
- Immediate 400–800 m controlled run

Use tall posture and avoid sprinting out of the station.

### Sled pull to run

- Sled pull
- 400 m smooth run
- Emphasize quick cadence and restored posture

### Burpee broad jumps to run

Progress total burpee volume carefully because the combination of increasing mileage, jumping and repeated trunk flexion can aggravate Achilles, knee or lumbar symptoms.

### Sandbag lunges to run

- 20–40 m loaded lunges
- Immediate 400–800 m run
- Shorter stride and higher cadence for the first 100–200 m

### Wall balls under fatigue

Practice repeatable set structures rather than fresh maximum sets:

- 20-15-15-10-10-10-10
- Sets of 15
- Sets of 10 with 5–8 controlled breaths

## Weeks 36–38 — Race-specific development

### Week 36

65–75% simulation:

- Six 1 km runs
- Six selected stations
- RPE 7–8
- Practice pacing and transitions

### Week 37

Broken simulation:

- Session one: four 1 km runs plus the first four stations
- Session two: final four stations with shortened running segments

### Week 38

Final major rehearsal, 12–16 days before race:

- 75–90% of the full race
- Race equipment and footwear
- Planned nutrition
- Planned station strategy
- No maximal finishing effort

## Weeks 39–40 — Taper and race

### Week 39

Reduce total volume by 35–45% while retaining:

- One short threshold exposure
- One light sled session
- One low-volume transition workout
- Two easy runs

### Race week

**Monday**

- 30 minutes easy
- Four 20-second strides
- Light upper body

**Tuesday**

Three rounds, not for time:

- 500 m at race pace
- 10 m sled push
- 10 m sled pull
- Eight wall balls

**Wednesday**

- 20–30 minutes easy
- Mobility

**Thursday**

- Rest or 15-minute shakeout

**Friday**

- Rest for a Saturday race
- Short shakeout for a Sunday race

**Competition**

Open the first kilometer slightly slower than target average pace. Strength-dominant athletes commonly overestimate how easy the first run feels.

# Methods Borrowed From High-Level HYROX Preparation

## Run frequently without racing every run

Advanced athletes accumulate substantial running volume because most sessions remain controlled. Build frequency through manageable runs rather than two destructive sessions per week.

## Separate pure running from compromised running

Pure running improves economy and threshold. Compromised running develops the ability to restore mechanics after stations. One or two compromised sessions per week is sufficient during specific preparation.

## Train station economy

Record and improve:

- Roxzone entry and exit
- Sled setup
- Rope handling
- Sandbag pickup
- Erg mounting and dismounting
- Wall-ball rest duration
- Failed-repetition rate

Small execution savings accumulate across the race.

## Alternate fast-running and fast-station emphasis

Use sessions where:

- Running is aggressive and stations are controlled
- Running is controlled and stations are aggressive

This develops tactical control rather than one indiscriminate pace.

## Build durability, not only VO2 max

Likely constraints for a large, strong athlete include:

- Running economy at higher body mass
- Lower-leg and hip tissue tolerance
- Clearance of local muscular fatigue
- Station efficiency
- Pace retention after sleds and lunges

## Fuel the workload

Do not combine peak HYROX volume with an aggressive caloric deficit. Maintain sufficient carbohydrate availability and monitor performance, sleep, resting heart rate and recurring pain.

# Weeks 41–60 — Bodybuilding Block

Week 60 is a physique competition, mock show or professionally staged photo-shoot peak.

## Phase structure

| Weeks | Phase | Primary objective |
|---:|---|---|
| 41–42 | Post-race recovery | Reduce fatigue and restore joints |
| 43–47 | Hypertrophy accumulation I | Build high-quality volume |
| 48 | Deload | Reduce fatigue |
| 49–53 | Hypertrophy accumulation II | Increase specialization |
| 54 | Deload and assessment | Recover and evaluate conditioning |
| 55–57 | Contest preparation | Maintain muscle while increasing the deficit |
| 58–59 | Peak preparation | Reduce damage and improve presentation |
| 60 | Competition | Peak week and show |

Running should be reduced gradually over six to eight weeks rather than removed immediately. Retaining some conditioning supports work capacity, recovery and appetite control.

# Bodybuilding Progression

## Weeks 41–42

- Two to three sets per movement
- Three to four repetitions in reserve
- No forced repetitions
- Two 30-minute easy aerobic sessions
- Restore appetite, sleep and joint comfort

## Weeks 43–47

Progress from approximately:

- 10–12 weekly sets per major muscle
- Toward 14–18 sets for priority muscles
- Eight to 12 sets for maintenance muscles

Most working sets remain at one to three repetitions in reserve.

## Week 48

- Reduce volume by 40–50%
- Maintain normal loads where comfortable
- No intensity techniques

## Weeks 49–53

Likely specialization priorities:

- Lateral delts
- Upper chest
- Lat width
- Hamstrings
- Calves
- Abdominal control

Use no more than one intensity method per body part per week:

- Rest-pause
- Drop set
- Lengthened partials
- Myo-reps

## Week 54

- Deload
- Assess body composition
- Confirm whether Week 60 is a judged contest, mock show or photo shoot
- Begin daily posing practice

## Weeks 55–57

- Maintain meaningful load
- Reduce unnecessary eccentric damage
- Increase low-intensity cardio only as needed
- Keep protein high
- Adjust calories from actual weekly weight trends

## Weeks 58–59

- No novel exercises
- No maximal lengthened-position soreness
- Reduce leg volume
- Maintain upper-body pump work
- Pose daily
- Rehearse trunks, tanning, transitions and mandatory poses

## Week 60

Avoid extreme dehydration or electrolyte manipulation. Peak-week decisions should prioritize muscle fullness, predictable digestion and safe presentation.

# Injury-Control System

## Green

- Symptoms 0–2/10
- No gait change
- Normal range of motion
- Symptoms settle during warm-up

Proceed as programmed.

## Yellow

- Symptoms 3–4/10
- Increasing morning stiffness
- Asymmetric running mechanics
- Performance declines for two sessions

Modify the week:

- Reduce running by 20–30%
- Reduce lower-body lifting sets by approximately 25%
- Reduce burpee and lunge volume
- Remove heavy sled exposure
- Replace one run with cycling or elliptical

## Red

- Sharp pain
- Altered gait
- Neurologic symptoms
- Night pain
- Rapidly declining hip range of motion
- Symptoms escalating across several days
- Inability to hop, jog or load the limb normally

Stop impact training and heavy lower-body work and obtain an in-person sports-medicine evaluation.

# Reference Systems

The strength-support model is influenced by Marcus Filly's Functional Bodybuilding approach:

- Tempo-controlled lifting
- Unilateral and contralateral exercises
- Structural balance
- Full-body hypertrophy
- Controlled mixed-modal conditioning
- Joint-friendly volume

Useful HYROX coaching and education sources to evaluate when refining this plan include:

- Official HYROX training resources and movement standards
- Roxzone Training
- RMR Training
- Rox Lyfe athlete and coaching interviews
- Centr HYROX programming

These references should inform methods, but athlete-specific volume must be governed by injury history, running background, race division, equipment access and recovery data.
